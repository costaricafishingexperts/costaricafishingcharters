        <a class="destination-card" href="https://www.costaricafishingexperts.com/flamingo-fishing-charters/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">
          <strong>Flamingo</strong>
          <p>Marina access near deeper water with billfish, tuna, dorado and inshore fishing options.</p>
        </a>
        <a class="destination-card" href="https://www.costaricafishingexperts.com/playa-jobo-fishing-charters/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">
          <strong>Playa Jobo</strong>
          <p>Northern Guanacaste option for travelers looking for a quieter base and mixed inshore/offshore fishing.</p>
        </a>
      </div>
    </div>

    <div class="tip"><strong>Trip-planning tip:</strong> If you have time for two fishing days, consider one inshore day and one offshore day to experience both sides of Costa Rica sportfishing.</div>
  </div>

<section class="section" id="species">
  <div class="wrap">
    <div class="section-head">
      <div class="eyebrow">What You Can Catch</div>
      <h2>Offshore & Inshore Species</h2>
      <p>Costa Rica is known for billfish offshore and powerful reef, rock and coastal species inshore.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="species-layout">
      <div class="species-image">
        <img src="https://www.costaricafishingexperts.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Roosterfish.jpeg"
             alt="Roosterfish caught while inshore fishing in Costa Rica"
             loading="lazy">
      </div>

      <div class="species-columns">
        <div class="species-card">
          <h3>Offshore All-Stars</h3>
          <ul>
            <li><strong>Blue Marlin:</strong> Big-game target around offshore current edges, seamounts and FAD routes.</li>
            <li><strong>Black Marlin:</strong> Powerful seasonal opportunities around bait-rich structure.</li>
            <li><strong>Striped Marlin:</strong> More associated with cooler-water influences and northern waters.</li>
            <li><strong>Pacific Sailfish:</strong> One of Costa Rica’s signature offshore species.</li>
            <li><strong>Dorado:</strong> Frequently associated with debris, current lines and seasonal green-water transitions.</li>
            <li><strong>Yellowfin Tuna:</strong> Often found with birds, dolphins and active bait schools.</li>
            <li><strong>Wahoo:</strong> Fast pelagic fish encountered around productive offshore structure.</li>
          </ul>
        </div>

        <div class="species-card">
          <h3>Inshore Icons</h3>
          <ul>
            <li><strong>Roosterfish:</strong> Famous Costa Rican inshore gamefish targeted around points, reefs and beaches.</li>
            <li><strong>Snapper:</strong> Cubera and other snapper species around reefs, wrecks and rocky structure.</li>
            <li><strong>Jack Crevalle:</strong> Aggressive schooling fish that provide hard-fighting light-tackle action.</li>
            <li><strong>Sierra Mackerel:</strong> Fast coastal species often targeted with smaller lures and light tackle.</li>
            <li><strong>Snook:</strong> Commonly associated with river mouths, estuaries and tidal zones.</li>
          </ul>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section soft" id="seasons">
  <div class="wrap">
    <div class="section-head">
      <div class="eyebrow">When to Go</div>
      <h2>Costa Rica Fishing Seasons</h2>
      <p>Fishing is possible year-round, but species concentrations and the best location can change throughout the year.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="season-grid">
      <div class="season-card">
        <strong>December – April</strong>
        <p>Classic Central Pacific sailfish period, with marlin opportunities and generally favorable dry-season travel conditions.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="season-card">
        <strong>May – August</strong>
        <p>Dorado and yellowfin tuna become important targets, with mixed billfish and strong opportunities to combine offshore and inshore days.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="season-card">
        <strong>September – November</strong>
        <p>Green-season weather is more variable, while dorado and tuna can remain productive and fishing pressure is often lower.</p>
      </div>
    </div>

    <div class="tip"><strong>Best approach:</strong> If your travel dates are fixed, choose the location that best matches the seasonal bite. If your hotel is fixed, tailor the target species and fishing style to that area.</div>
  </div>
</section>

      <h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
    </div>

    <div class="faq">
      <details>
        <summary>Is fishing good in Costa Rica year-round?</summary>
        <p>Yes. The strongest target species and best regions change throughout the year, so trips should be matched to your dates and location.</p>
      </details>

      <details>
        <summary>Which Costa Rica fishing location is best?</summary>
        <p>It depends on timing and goals. The Central Pacific is famous for billfish, the Southern Pacific offers a wilder setting and varied fishing, and Guanacaste combines resort areas with offshore and inshore opportunities.</p>
      </details>

      <details>
        <summary>Do I need a Costa Rica fishing license?</summary>
        <p>Yes. Costa Rica requires fishing licenses. Confirm the current purchase process before your trip.</p>
      </details>

      <details>
        <summary>Are fishing charters kid-friendly?</summary>
        <p>Many are. Inshore trips, calmer morning conditions, shade and boats with additional comfort features can make a big difference for families.</p>
      </details>

      <details>
        <summary>What happens if weather changes?</summary>
        <p>Captains may modify the plan for safety and comfort. Depending on conditions, that can mean changing fishing areas or adjusting the target species.</p>
      </details>

      <details>
        <summary>What is a customary crew tip?</summary>
        <p>The source material recommends approximately 15–20% of the charter rate depending on service and results.</p>
